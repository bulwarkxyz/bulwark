## A rule

A rule is a trigger and one or more actions, optionally limited to a fixed window:

| Triggers | Actions |
|---|---|
| Buffer below a line | Alert |
| Account down by a share since the window opened or since you signed | Move idle USDC into the pool (the amount you set) |
| A market moving up or down by a share | Cut a position by a share, or close it |
| A position's leverage above a level | Trim until the buffer is back at a line, or a market back to a leverage |
| | Cancel orders that would add to a position |

Windows are a fixed table, not numbers: **weekend** (Friday US close to Monday US open), **overnight** (US close to the next US open) and **US session** (9:30–16:00 New York time on weekdays).

## Every number is yours

The app never pre-fills a number and never suggests one. Every line, share and amount in your rules is a number you typed.

## The AI translator

You can write a rule in a sentence, for example *"close gold if it falls [your number]% overnight"*. An AI model (Claude, by Anthropic) turns it into one exact rule. The app labels it as an AI translator.

- **What it sees:** your sentence, the market list and your current rules. No keys, and no access to the exchange.
- **Numbers:** every number in a draft must be one you typed; otherwise the draft is refused before you see it.
- **Only adds protection:** a draft can add a rule. It cannot change, loosen or remove an existing rule or your slippage limit. Loosening is a manual edit with its own signature.
- **Asks or refuses:** if a number, market or action is missing, it asks one question. If the request isn't a protective rule (raising leverage, opening positions, sending funds), it refuses.
- **What you see:** the rule shown back to you is generated from the rule's structure, not the model's wording, so every number on screen comes from the rule.
- **You sign it:** nothing applies until you sign the new policy version in your wallet.

  The translator is built and tested with a stand-in model; it switches on in the testnet preview when its API key is configured. Until then, use the stage editor.

## Signing a policy

A policy is your full set of rules plus your slippage limit, with a version number. You sign its hash (EIP-712) in your wallet. The guard acts only under the policy whose hash matches your stored signature (check I4).
