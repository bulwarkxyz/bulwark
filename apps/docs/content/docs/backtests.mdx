## How each replay is run

The replay feeds recorded prices through the **same code the guard runs**: the margin model, the rule evaluation, the seven checks and the guard's own actions, step by step.

**The account.** A cross long on the xyz dex, opened at the first price of each window, with 10,000 USDC of equity and nothing else in the account.

**Leverage.** 3×, 5×, 10× and 20× (up to each market's maximum).

**Stage settings: example settings chosen for the test.** They are not product defaults; in the app, every number is one you set.

| Stage | When | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buffer below 2× | Trim until the buffer is back at 3× |
| 2 | Buffer below 1.5× | Trim until the buffer is back at 2.5× |
| 3 | Buffer below 1.2× | Close the position |

**The same account with no guard**, on the same prices, is shown next to every result.

**Fees and slippage.** Guard orders fill at the worst price a 1% slippage limit allows, and pay a fee of 4.5 bps per fill (assumed).

**Congestion.** Each scenario is run with the guard's orders reaching the exchange immediately, 1 minute late and 5 minutes late. A late order is an IOC with the limit it had when it was decided: if the price has already moved past that limit, it does not fill.

**Margin.** Current xyz maintenance tiers; the tiers on those days may have differed.

## What this does not prove

- **A replay is not a live cascade.** In a real cascade, liquidity disappears, order books thin out, and other traders and liquidations move the price in response to each other. A replay moves along recorded prices that our orders do not affect.
- **Congestion is modelled only as a delay.** The 1- and 5-minute cases delay the guard's orders; they do not model a slow or unavailable Hyperliquid API, rate limits, queue position or partial fills.
- **Fills are assumed, not observed.** Orders fill at the slippage limit if the price allows; depth is not checked.
- **Funding is not included.**
- **The settings are examples.** Different lines and actions give different results, better or worse. They are not advice and not product defaults.
- **Surviving is not the same as keeping your money.** The guard can avoid the liquidation event and still end with a small share of the equity, and a trim can lock in a loss before a recovery.

## Reproduce

The script, its inputs and these settings are in the public repository:

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm build
pnpm --filter @bulwarkxyz/ops backtest                     # stored inputs in data/backtests/
pnpm --filter @bulwarkxyz/ops backtest -- --refresh        # re-fetch trade candles from Hyperliquid
pnpm --filter @bulwarkxyz/ops backtest -- --mark-dir DIR   # mark prices: DIR/<case>.csv with block_minute,mark_price
```

Each run writes its results and every assumption to `evidence/backtest-<source>-<date>.{json,md}`.
