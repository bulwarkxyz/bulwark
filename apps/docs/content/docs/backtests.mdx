We replayed three recent crash days through the guard's own code, on **mark prices**, the prices Hyperliquid uses for margin, liquidations and stops. Every result is below, good and bad.

<Callout title="Where the numbers come from">
  - **From [Hydromancer](https://hydromancer.xyz):** every price path on this page. Oil and SK hynix use the accepted mark price of each oracle round (`perpPriceHistoryByTime`, about one round every 3 seconds). Silver uses the mark price the market's deployer submitted (`oraclePriceHistoryByTime`), because accepted mark rounds for HIP-3 markets are recorded only from 24 February 2026. The comparison of submitted and accepted marks below is computed from Hydromancer data too.
  - **From Hyperliquid directly:** each market's maintenance-margin tiers, maximum leverage and lot sizes (`perpDexs` and `allPerpMetas`), read when the replay runs.
  - **Ours:** the margin model, the rule evaluation, the seven checks and the guard's actions: the same code the guard runs, open source.

  Mark-price history courtesy of Hydromancer.
</Callout>

| 23 March 2026 | `xyz:CL` (oil) | Accepted mark, 85,311 rounds | −15.4% (10:28 → 11:09 UTC) | −14.1% |
| 27 July 2026 | `xyz:SKHX` (SK hynix) | Accepted mark, 86,386 rounds | −19.4% (22:01 → 23:01 UTC) | −25.0% |

Each window runs three days, from 00:00 UTC on the day before to 00:00 UTC on the day after.

**Data quality.**
- **Oil and SK hynix** have no gap longer than 8 seconds.
- **Silver** has one gap of 106 seconds, at 08:03 UTC on 31 January, after the crash.
- **Silver is the weaker input.** It is the deployer's *submitted* mark, not the mark Hyperliquid accepted. On the two days where we have both, the submitted mark was close on oil: median 0.05% from the accepted mark, 0.5% at the 99th percentile, 1.4% at most. On SK hynix it was much further: median 0.17%, 2.2% at the 99th percentile and up to 15% during the wick. Read the silver results with that in mind.

**SK hynix: the wick was in the mark price too.** We had marked this day inconclusive while we had only trade prices, because the drop was a bad print in trades. On mark prices it is conclusive. The accepted mark fell from about 1,128 to 917 (−18.7%) between 23:00 and 23:01 UTC and was back above 1,150 by 23:03. Liquidations run on mark, so they would have happened.

## How each replay is run

The replay feeds the recorded prices through **the same code the guard runs**, one price round at a time: the margin model, the rule evaluation, the seven checks and the guard's own actions.

**The account.** A cross long on the xyz dex, opened at the first price of each window, with 10,000 USDC of equity and nothing else in the account.

**Leverage.** 3×, 5×, 10× and 20×, up to each market's maximum: 25× for silver, 20× for oil, 10× for SK hynix.

<Callout type="warn">
  At a market's maximum leverage, an account starts with a buffer of exactly 2×, the first example line below, so the guard trims straight away. That applies to oil at 20× and SK hynix at 10×.
</Callout>

**Stage settings: example settings chosen for the test.** They are not product defaults; in the app, every number is one you set.

| Stage | When | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buffer below 2× | Trim until the buffer is back at 3× |
| 2 | Buffer below 1.5× | Trim until the buffer is back at 2.5× |
| 3 | Buffer below 1.2× | Close the position |

**The same account with no guard**, on the same prices, is shown next to every result.

**Fees and slippage.** Guard orders fill at the worst price a 1% slippage limit allows, and pay a fee of 4.5 bps per fill (assumed).

**On time and late.** "On time" means the guard acts on the same price round it sees, about every 3 seconds. Live, we measured about 94 ms from a price tick to the exchange’s answer, from our server in Singapore. Each scenario is also run with every guard order reaching the exchange **1 minute** and **5 minutes** late. A late order is an IOC with the limit it had when it was decided: if the price has already moved past that limit, it does not fill. As in the live guard, no new decision is made for the account while an order is still on its way.

**Retries.** An order that did not fill, or filled only partly, is sent again once its result is known, while its stage still holds. Each retry is re-priced from the mark at that moment and stays within the same 1% slippage. The guard's 20-actions-a-minute cap applies. Each late scenario is run with and without retries ([how retries work](/how-the-guard-works#when-an-order-does-not-fill)).

**Margin.** Current xyz maintenance tiers; the tiers on those days may have differed.

## What the results show

- **On time, the guard avoided every liquidation, but often kept little.** The unguarded account was liquidated in 8 of the 11 scenarios. With the guard acting on time, none of those 8 was. What they kept at the end of the window ranged from **$0.19 to $2,480** of $10,000.
- **On silver it avoided liquidation but kept almost nothing.** At 5×, 10× and 20× it ended with $3.83, $0.19 and $0.30. With these example lines, the guard held the account near its lines all the way down. Avoiding liquidation is not the same as keeping your money.
- **Late, it mostly failed.** With orders 1 minute late, only silver at 3× avoided liquidation. With orders 5 minutes late, none did. On oil, the mark fell more than 8% in under three minutes (98.6 at 11:05 to 90.4 at 11:07:37 UTC), faster than a late order could arrive.
- **Retries changed no result on these days.** In all 16 late runs where a stage fired, the result with retries matched the result without. The late runs ended in liquidation because the account got from its first line to liquidation faster than the delay, not because orders kept missing. Retries help when the price pauses or bounces between attempts; these three paths did not do that at the moments that counted.
- **Where no line was crossed, the guard did nothing.** Oil at 3× and 5×, and SK hynix at 3×, end exactly as the unguarded account.

## What this does not prove

- **A replay is not a live cascade.** In a real cascade, liquidity disappears, order books thin out, and other traders and liquidations move the price in response to each other. A replay moves along recorded prices that our orders do not affect.
- **Congestion is modelled only as a delay.** The 1- and 5-minute cases delay the guard's orders. They do not model a slow or unavailable Hyperliquid API, rate limits from other traffic, queue position or partial fills.
- **Fills are assumed, not observed.** Orders fill at the slippage limit if the price allows; depth is not checked.
- **Silver runs on the submitted mark,** which can differ from the mark Hyperliquid accepted (see Data quality above).
- **Funding is not included,** and today's margin tiers are used for past days.
- **The settings are examples.** Different lines and actions give different results, better or worse. They are not advice and not product defaults.
- **Surviving is not the same as keeping your money.** The guard can avoid the liquidation and still end with almost nothing, and a trim can lock in a loss before a recovery.
- **Three days are three days.** They were chosen because they were sharp, recent HIP-3 crashes, not to show the guard at its best or worst.

## Reproduce

The script and these settings are in the public repository. The Hydromancer price data is not redistributed: the script fetches it with your own [Hydromancer API key](https://hydromancer.xyz) and caches it locally.

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm build
HYDROMANCER_API_KEY=… pnpm --filter @bulwarkxyz/ops backtest -- --hydromancer   # the results on this page
pnpm --filter @bulwarkxyz/ops backtest -- --mark-dir DIR   # your own mark prices: DIR/<case>.csv with block_minute,mark_price
pnpm --filter @bulwarkxyz/ops backtest                     # hourly trade prices stored in data/backtests/ (coarse)
```

Each run writes its results and every assumption to `evidence/backtest-<source>-<date>.{json,md}`. The results above are `evidence/backtest-mark-2026-10-05`.
